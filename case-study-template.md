## The situation

What was true when you walked in. Keep it short and factual. No scene setting.

## The constraint

The thing that made this hard. Not "we had a tight deadline" — the actual
structural problem: a regulation, a dependency you didn't control, a metric
that couldn't move without another one falling.

This section is what makes it a case study rather than a summary. If there is
no real constraint, this is not the story to tell.

## What I decided

The call, stated plainly, in one or two sentences. Before the reasoning.

## What I rejected and why

Two or three alternatives you seriously considered, and the reason each lost.
Most portfolios skip this. It is the part people actually read, because it is
the only part that shows judgement rather than outcome.

Be honest about the option that was nearly right.

## How it played out

The result, in relative terms only. See the disclosure rule in the README.

Include what went wrong. Something did.

## What I'd do differently

Not false modesty. A specific thing you now know that you didn't then.
