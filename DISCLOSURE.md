# Disclosure rules

Everything published here follows one rule.

**Relative movement is safe. Absolute business volume is not.**

Naming Flutter and Junglee Games is fine — that is public on LinkedIn — as long
as it is never paired with an absolute figure.

## Approved phrasing

Use the language on the right. It is already normalised, and it is the only
version that appears anywhere in this repo.

| Instead of an absolute | Write |
|---|---|
| Monthly gross profit, start and end | grew roughly 20x over three years |
| Activation rate, before and after | from under half to roughly three quarters |
| Customer count on a campaign | over a million customers |
| Per-brand D7 retention rates | D7 retention around 40 percent |
| Checkout success point change | a double digit lift in checkout success |
| Purchases per user delta | a step change in purchases per user |
| Revenue per user delta at D7 | high single digit RPU lift |
| Daily active participation change | roughly a 5 point lift in a month |
| ARPU delta | up about seven percent / up about two and a half percent |
| Share of financed transactions routed | over 40 percent |

Percentage-point deltas with no base disclosed are safe as written — a reader
cannot reconstruct volume from them. Multiples are safe. Ratios between two
test arms are safe.

## Never in this repo, in any file

- Absolute revenue, gross profit, ARPU, NGR, rake or wager figures
- App-open counts, cohort sizes, user counts, depositor counts
- Per-brand retention rates
- Colleague names, Jira or epic IDs, Figma links, internal Drive links
- Unshipped variants that were never publicly released
- The resume PDFs — they carry absolute figures by design, and belong in a
  recruiter's inbox rather than in a public repository

## Images

Before anything lands in `assets/`, black out player handles, avatars, account
balances, personal promo codes and referral amounts. Check the whole image, not
just the header — promo codes sit inside banners and referral values sit in the
bottom nav.

## Attribution

Every case study states what I owned and what partner teams owned. Where a
conclusion is my reading of the data rather than something the analysis stated
outright, it says so inline.
